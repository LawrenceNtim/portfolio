User research · the pivot that reframed v0
Positioning

An assistant, not a planner.

Everyone in AI travel wants to be the autonomous agent. But only 2% of people will let AI book on their behalf. The autonomous door is crowded and distrusted. Wing takes the other one.

Planners Promise to do everything Mindtrip, Layla, Hopper. Over-promise autonomy, fail at the edges, and lose trust the moment the user feels out of control.
Assistants Augment your judgment Ask the right clarifying question, surface the trade-offs, and stay useful even when imperfect. Agency stays reversible at every step.
Wing The twin who knows you Does the work you'd do yourself — applies what it already knows, recommends with reasoning, hands you the controls.

The moat

~17 preference axes. The specificity is the moat.

Tier 1 · onboarding · ~90s
cabin · international cabin · domestic seat · window/aisle deal-breakers card portfolio companions time of day

5–7 questions, captured up front. Enough to make the first search feel like it already knows you.

Tier 2 · progressive · 10+

Captured when relevant — never as one form. The longer and weirder the list, the harder to copy.

Anyone can claim "we remember preferences." Wing tracking "no MAX 8s, prefer A350s, Fido is 45 lbs and cabin-eligible" is the demonstrable difference. Memory is the moat.

The design IP

Most AI gives you one answer with a reasoning expander. We rejected the pattern.

The dominant pattern in AI search — Perplexity, AI Overviews, Copilot — is one recommendation you have to trust. That hides the work. Wing shows the full decision matrix: cash × points × economy × business, in parallel.

Cross-stack comparison surfaces the trade-off. A Bottom Line synthesizes it without forcing a single answer. The grid is adaptive — one stack for a simple domestic hop, four when the decision is genuinely rich.

This was the highest-leverage design decision in the spec.

One line that changes everything

The moat is invisible until you show the work.

Without this line, Wing looks like a beautiful Google Flights. With it, Wing is an assistant that did work for you before you ever scrolled.

Trust + control

The matrix surfaces options. Intervention points let you steer.

When the agent is mid-task, how does the user redirect without losing its state? Make "stop and ask me" feel safe, not disruptive. This is the layer behind the matrix — and the same shape as conversational AI Search.

Redirect mid-search; agent keeps everything it already found.

b · safe interruption

A clarifying question that doesn't throw away progress.

c · overruled decisions

Every autonomous action stays intervenable, undoable, auditable.

Architecture

Preferences are the moat — not the model.

Wing is MCP-based, so any model can plug in. The patterns I'm designing — intervention surfaces, state management, trust signals — are designed once and don't break when the underlying model changes.

That's infrastructure-aware design: I'm not designing the AI at the surface layer, I'm designing the system that orchestrates what the user wants, what the agent does, and how they negotiate control.

Claude Code, Cursor, MCP — part of my design medium now, not a constraint.

The build · which servers, and why

Eight specialized servers. One orchestrator.

Each server is developed, tested, and improved independently; the intelligence layer parses intent, routes in parallel, and aggregates the results into one coherent answer. Modularity is the competitive advantage.

How it gets made

A designer working at the infrastructure layer.

Built solo across Claude Code, Cursor, and Codex — Linear sprints, weekly end-to-end demos, deployed continuously on Render and Vercel. The same loop a product team runs, compressed to one person.

Real data, always
Every temptation to hardcode "for now" creates debt. SerpAPI and Seats.aero return real prices and real award space.
Nightmare scenarios
Test against the hardest realistic case — multi-city, pets, points across three programs — not happy paths. If that works, simple cases take care of themselves.
Fail transparently
When a server is down, say so. Users making financial decisions on fabricated award space is the worst possible outcome.
Meeting users where they are

One infrastructure. Two surfaces.

The same Wing MCP powers a conversation inside an LLM and a focused web app. Wing the surface is one expression; Wing the infrastructure is what makes it portable.

What it taught me
01
Pivot in the open

Name the shift as real, not a refinement. Update the spec, then let the new thesis flow downstream into every UI decision.
02
Specificity is the moat

"Personalized travel" is copyable. "No MAX 8s, prefer A350s, Fido 45 lbs cabin-eligible" is not. The longer and weirder the list, the more defensible.
03
Orchestration over pixels

The hard design problems aren't on the surface — they're in what gets orchestrated, in what order, with what visibility. Pixels are downstream of those calls.
04
Trust is the gating constraint

Every consequential AI surface is a trust problem first and a feature problem second. Make agency reversible, and never fabricate at the data layer.
Where the puck is going
