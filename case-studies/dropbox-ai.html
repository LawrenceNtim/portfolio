The Build · Surface 01

Where AI lives — and where it doesn't. Iterations, guiding the pattern.

Iteration 01
AI Right Rail.

A persistent panel that lived alongside files. Discoverable but heavy. A constant reminder that AI was watching.

Iteration 02
Search Toggle.

AI as a mode of search. Closer to user intent, but the toggle was a tax we kept making them pay.

Shipped
Result.

AI inside the result, not adjacent to it. Available where the user was already looking. Never asked to switch contexts.

The Build · Surface 01 / Why Result won

The shipped pattern doesn't ask permission. It just answers.

01

The user's task is the surface.

If you came in looking for a file, the AI answer arrives where the file would have. No new place to learn, no new place to dismiss.

02

AI generated summaries.

Files are summarized when searches return, giving users maximum view into their files, quickly and easily.

03

The follow-up is the same surface.

Refining a question doesn't relocate the user. The conversation deepens in place. No chat panel, no context switch.

The Build · Surface 02

Available, not in
the way.

Half the user base wanted AI yesterday. The other half didn't want their files anywhere near a model. The same product had to read as both, without picking a side.

The answer was disclosure, not promotion. AI showed up the first time you'd benefit from it, never the moment you opened the app. After that, it sat where it sat. Quiet by default, summoned on demand.

The Build · Surface 03

AI is wrong sometimes. Making that clear and approachable.

Workflow efficiency is paramount for Dropbox, it's critical to find ways that AI can help maintain flow state.

The Build · Surface 04 / Response

The forty-eight hour rebuild.

The toggle wasn't broken. The disclosure was. The team rewrote AI Settings from the user's reading level up: what's on, what's off, what each thing does in one sentence.

We promoted third-party AI to the top of the panel. Added an in-product link from every AI surface so the answer to "where is this controlled" was always one click away. Replaced legalese with plain English, reviewed by Erin and signed off directly by Drew.

Shipped 48 hours after the first thread. The conversation didn't stop, but it moved on.

Designing under public pressure with humility and understanding.

The Build · AI Guidelines

Six principles, committed to.

When the trust crisis ended, we extracted what we'd learned into a set of guidelines we now design every AI feature against. Co-Authors: PM, Legal.

01

Leverage AI to serve our customers.

We use AI when it helps us deliver better experiences for our customers. At no point will we use it as a means to sell customer data.

02

Keep customers in control of their data.

Customer trust and the privacy of their data are our foundation. We will not use customer data to train AI models without consent.

03

Be transparent about how we use AI.

We are committed to transparency with our customers. We will provide clear explanations of how our AI experiences work so that our customers understand how these technologies can benefit them.

04

Respect people, their safety, and their rights.

We will work to ensure that our AI innovations not only serve people, but also respect their rights and their safety.

05

Be accountable to our customers.

We will continuously seek feedback from our customers about our AI-powered experiences, and ensure these experiences remain under human direction.

06

Champion fairness in AI technology.

We are committed to inclusiveness, non-discrimination, and fairness. We will strive to limit bias in our AI technologies, and ensure that they are
reliable and robust.

The Process · Research

Research wasn't a phase. It was the floor we built on.

In partnership with DBX Research, we ran two studies before any pixels left the canvas: one on what users wanted from AI inside Dropbox, one on whether they'd opt in.

The second study is what told us the toggle had to be off by default. We didn't get there from gut. We got there from 22 pages of customer conversation about willingness, disclosure, and trust.

The Reflection · The Lessons

Four things I learned in public, that I do differently now.

01
Over-communicate.

When the work is novel, the team is small, and the timeline is tight, the bottleneck isn't decisions, it's shared context. I now write the working brief weekly, post the open questions before standup, and treat "did I say this twice" as a feature, not a tax.

02
Tighter team loops.

Big XFN reviews are the wrong unit of work for AI. I run smaller, faster, three-person loops now (designer, PM, engineer) and bring the wider room in only when there's a decision to make, not a decision to socialize.

03
New patterns, on purpose.

AI products invite reuse of chat patterns that don't fit the work. I now start every AI surface with the user's existing task and ask what shape AI takes inside that task. Not what shape AI takes when it's the task itself.

04
Even more HCD.

Trust is a research problem before it's a UI problem. I now bring research into AI work earlier than feels comfortable, and I push the bar on disclosure and consent design specifically, because the cost of getting it wrong compounds faster than any other surface.
