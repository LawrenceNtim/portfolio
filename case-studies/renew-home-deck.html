The problem

Today, you are the orchestrator.

Booking a trip well means optimizing fifteen variables at once — cabin, seat, connection risk, cash vs. award, status, transfer ratios, aircraft, lounges, weather, timing — across ten-plus tools that don't talk to each other.

You query, you filter, you compare, you decide, you book. The cognitive load is the product. AI can collapse it — but only if the design problem gets solved.

Architecture

Preferences are the moat — not the model.

Wing is MCP-based, so any model can plug in. The patterns I'm designing — intervention surfaces, state management, trust signals — are designed once and don't break when the underlying model changes.

That's infrastructure-aware design: I'm not designing the AI at the surface layer, I'm designing the system that orchestrates what the user wants, what the agent does, and how they negotiate control.

Claude Code, Cursor, MCP — part of my design medium now, not a constraint.

Process · Framing

Every existing way to get a card into a checkout was built for a different job.

Card entry has been treated as a single problem with multiple solutions. It isn't.

Method Designed for Card-present at enrollment First-txn auth rate Fraud liability CVC User effort
Manual entryPAN + exp + CVC + ZIP Trust by typing No Standard CNP Merchant Required 16 digits, expiry, CVC, billing ZIP — typed by hand
Card scan (OCR)Camera-based autofill Faster manual entry No Standard CNP Merchant Required Camera capture + manual CVC + verification fields
Apple / Google PayAdd-card / provisioning Wallet provisioning No Standard CNP at this merchant Merchant Not at checkout Card must already be entered into the wallet via CNP path
Tap to PayStripe Terminal · for context Merchant accepting payment Yes Higher (CP) Issuer Not required Same primitive — but pointed at the wrong moment for enrollment
Tap to AddMobile Payment Element Enrolling a card for future use Yes Higher (CP) Issuer Not required One physical gesture — tap card to phone
Tap to Add is the only method that wins on every dimension simultaneously. Card-present enrollment collapses the tradeoff.
Process · Reposition

Same primitive. Different moment.

Terminal's Tap to Pay was built for merchants — hardware, cryptogram, and auth path all pointed at the moment of charging.

We pointed it at the moment of saving. Same infrastructure — different purpose.

Infrastructure can be repointed, not just built.

Process · Device variance

The tap zone moves. Across every Android phone.

Apple ships every iPhone with the NFC antenna in one known place. Android doesn't. Pixel, Samsung, OnePlus, Motorola — every OEM picks a different antenna location. Top, middle, edge. No shared convention, no public lookup.

A single tap animation would land on the wrong spot every time. The buyer holds the phone to nothing. The interaction stops feeling real.

Process · The solution

Device-aware. Anchored at runtime.

Worked with Terminal's hardware team on a device → NFC zone lookup. The animation's anchor adapts at runtime to the buyer's specific phone — top for Pixel, middle for OnePlus, wherever the actual antenna sits.

The prompt always lands on the real zone for the device. The buyer's tap finds something to land on.

Release and Impact · Cross-team

Terminal's screen was for merchants. Ours wasn't.

Left alone, buyers in MPE would land on Terminal's merchant screen — a non-device-aware animation, a confusing "X," a visual language built for a different product.

Three buyer-side fixes went into the gaveled designs: a device-aware NFC animation, an "Enter card info manually" exit, and an "X" that returns to payment methods.

Cross-team alignment was the highest-leverage work I did on this project.

Release and Impact · Preview constraints

What private preview meant. And what it didn't.

Three known tradeoffs accepted at private preview, documented for GA.

01 · SDK Size

20–30MB at preview. Target: under 2MB at GA.

A lightweight NFC fallback is under exploration for merchants who can't take the full Terminal SDK.

02 · Bifurcated Tap Screen

"Enter card info manually" deferred to GA.

Time before Sessions ruled out the bifurcated screen. Fallback: Tap to Add is no longer the default when the buyer selects "Card."

03 · Design Language

Working group standing up.

Terminal Design + OCS Mobile alignment is scoped and continues into GA — starting with Phil Machalski, broadening to the dashboard team.

Release and Impact · Private preview · Apr 29, 2026

Android. Private preview. Live.

Tap to Add launched in private preview on April 29, 2026 inside the Mobile Payment Element on Android. Documented publicly at docs.stripe.com/payments/mobile/embedded-tap-to-add.

Merchants invited into the preview can integrate today; iOS support depends on Apple approving the use case, with conversations re-opening once Android proves the value.

Release and Impact · Public preview · Mid Q2 2025

Android. Public preview.

Public preview broadens the rollout beyond the initial merchant set. Behavioral telemetry tuning, expanded device matrix coverage, and failure-state polish all land here.

Walk the live prototype to see where the buyer-facing flow is heading next.

Release and Impact · GA · Later in 2026

Android. GA.

GA lands the third buyer-side recommendation held from private preview and the tap-screen visual language we now own across the Mobile Payment Element.

Brand, Comms, and GTM ride alongside — the surface is ours, end to end.

Lessons · The Lessons

The Lessons.

01
Infrastructure can be repointed, not just built.

The Tap to Pay stack was already there. The work was seeing what else it could do.

02
The strongest design moves are economic, not visual.

The interaction looks simple. The auth-rate, fraud, and liability shifts behind it are anything but.

03
Sole designer doesn't mean solo work.

Cross-team alignment with Terminal was the highest-leverage thing I did this project.

04
Private preview is a discipline.

You ship the right slice, document what's deferred, and protect the work from scope creep.
