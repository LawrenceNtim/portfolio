DocSend Top-of-Funnel Problem Statement

DocSend's funnel converts a fraction of the visitors it brings in. Activation drops sharply at every step. What's the path to a self-sustaining business?

User Problem

Users land on DocSend ready to send a deck or build a data room. The path from landing page to activated user is 12 steps long and drops 90% of users along the way.

We believe that building a better onboarding and activation experience for DocSend users will result in an increase in free-to-paid conversion. We will know we are successful when activation rate moves from <10% to a defensible benchmark.

Research.

Setting the team up for success with dedicated research. Understanding what the key activation problems actually were before building anything.

DocSend: Top of Funnel Research Brief
Q2 2024 · DocSend & DBX Research
TLDR

Activation drops sharply at every step of the funnel. Of the visitors who reach the site, fewer than 10% reach activation. The biggest drops are concentrated on day 0.

Non-founder personas activate ~18% lower than founders. The product was implicitly designed for one persona; the funnel was bringing in others.

Key Findings

88% of activations happen on day 0. If a user doesn't activate the day they sign up, they very likely never will.

Sign-up is the highest drop step: confusion about the auto-trial, lack of context, and the modal entry pattern all compound.

Checkout requires 3 page navigations to change plan details. Users abandon when they realize they can't see the whole picture.

Solutions · The Whole Flow

Illustrating the whole flow.

12 steps from landing page to first time in the product, with significant drop-off through the whole process.

Mapping every step end to end made the redesign tractable. Instead of fixing pages, we were fixing transitions.

Solutions · Sign Up

Sign up: Before.

Sign up was surfaced by a modal with information split across steps. Users were dropped into a trial with no context for what it was or when it would end.

The biggest single drop in the funnel sat here.

Solutions · Sign Up

Sign up: After.

Surfaced the most important information first. Gave the auto-trial an explicit start, midpoint reminder, and end date. Removed the modal, gave the page room to breathe.

"Experience the best of DocSend free for 14 days," said up front, instead of inferred from a banner three pages in.

Designed with Treyce, special guest.

Solutions · Survey

Survey: Before.

Multiple steps, and a visual language that didn't match Dropbox. Users coming in from Dropbox surfaces hit it and bounced. The change in design felt like leaving the product.

Solutions · Survey

Survey: After.

Bridged DocSend and Dropbox's design systems by hand. DocSend wasn't resourced with DSE support, so this was a one-person port. Consolidated steps, used Dropbox-native components.

A user coming from Dropbox sees a continuous experience instead of a context switch.

Solutions · Checkout

Checkout: Before.

Multiple steps, no modular controls. Changing plan details meant going back three pages, and starting over if you got it wrong.

Solutions · Checkout

Checkout: After.

Condensed to one screen, modular, with inline editability. Plan details, team size, and payment all visible at once.

A new pattern for the team: billing surfaces that don't lock the user into a path.

Solutions · Activation

New activation. Personalized next step.

"Where would you like to start, Lawrence?" The last step in onboarding gives the user a choice of paths, relevant to their persona, not the founder-only default.

Both founder and non-founder paths feel built for them.

Solutions · Marketing Site

Existing site.

DocSend.com as it stood: pre-acquisition design, gradually patched but never reframed. Visitors couldn't quickly tell what DocSend was for, or where to go.

Solutions · Marketing Site

Running a design sprint.

Partnered with Dropbox Brand to run a one-week sprint on the new site. The goal: give DocSend visual focus and convert the cold visitor faster.

Designed with Dropbox Brand & Principal Designers.

Solutions · Marketing Site

Refreshed site.

Fully refreshed DocSend.com. New hero, sharper positioning, and a path to sign-up that doesn't bury the value proposition.

Solutions · Bonus

Bonus credit: Emails.

Took initiative to refresh DocSend's most important communication channel. Emails are how users learn that their deck has been opened, the moment that drives most return visits.

Cleaner hierarchy, clearer subject, a single CTA. Designed for skim.
